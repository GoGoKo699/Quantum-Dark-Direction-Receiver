# Exact collective minimax risk

A source in $`\mathbb C^d`$ emits $`N`$ independent copies of $`\rho_f=(I-P_f)/(d-1)`$, where $`P_f=|f\rangle\langle f|`$ projects onto a wholly unknown ray. The occupied subspace has uniform population. A receiver reports a ray $`g`$ and incurs infidelity $`1-\operatorname{tr}(P_gP_f)`$. The laboratory basis is known, every outcome is included, and the measurement is ideal. For the separate qutrit storage and calibration models, see [the model](model.md).

For $`d\ge 2`$ and $`N\ge 0`$, the exact collective minimax risk is

```math
R_{\mathrm{coll}}^*(N,d)
=1-\sum_{\substack{\lambda\vdash N\\ \lambda_d=0}}
\frac{r_\lambda m_\lambda}{(d-1)^N}
\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\frac{\lambda_i+d-i}{\lambda_i+d-i+1},
\qquad
r_\lambda=\dim S_\lambda(\mathbb C^{d-1}).
```

The sum uses partitions with at most $`d-1`$ rows, padded to length $`d`$. Here $`S_\lambda`$ is the Schur functor and $`m_\lambda`$ is the dimension of the symmetric-group representation of shape $`\lambda`$. The formula combines the exact probability of each Schur sector with the optimal conditional fidelity in that sector.

The optimum is attainable by a finite POVM for every $`N,d`$. For $`d\ge3`$, measuring specimens individually attains the same optimum through $`N=d-1`$. At $`N=d`$, the optimum strictly improves, and a POVM whose every effect is biseparable cannot attain it. At fixed $`d`$, the optimal risk satisfies $`N R_{\mathrm{coll}}^*(N,d)\longrightarrow (d-1)^2`$.

## 1. Source and score in each Schur sector

Write $`V_\lambda=S_\lambda(\mathbb C^d)`$, $`D_\lambda=\dim V_\lambda`$, and let $`M_\lambda`$ be the multiplicity space of dimension $`m_\lambda`$. In the Schur–Weyl decomposition, the source is

```math
\rho_f^{\otimes N}
=\frac{1}{(d-1)^N}
\bigoplus_{\lambda:\lambda_d=0}\Pi_\lambda(f)\otimes I_{M_\lambda},
\qquad
\Pi_\lambda(f)=S_\lambda(I-P_f).
```

The functor applied to the orthogonal projection onto $`f^\perp`$ gives the orthogonal projection onto the corresponding $`S_\lambda(f^\perp)`$ of rank $`r_\lambda`$. This follows by restricting the tensor-power projection to the Schur decomposition. All blocks with $`\lambda_d>0`$ are zero on the source. In particular the displayed weights sum to one.

Use Haar ray measure of total mass one. A guess represented by a rank-one projector $`H`$ has fidelity score operator

```math
B_H=\int \operatorname{tr}(HP_f)\rho_f^{\otimes N}\,df .
```

For a POVM $`E(dg)`$, its Haar mean fidelity is the integral of $`\operatorname{tr}[E(dg)B_{P_g}]`$. This upper-bounds its worst-case fidelity. In each supported Schur sector, introduce the linear map

```math
T_H^\lambda=\frac{D_\lambda}{r_\lambda}
\int \operatorname{tr}(HP_f)\Pi_\lambda(f)\,df .
```

When $`H=I`$, Schur's lemma gives $`T_I^\lambda=I_{V_\lambda}`$. We will show that for each rank-one $`H`$, this map has the largest eigenvalue

```math
s_d=\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\frac{\lambda_i+d-i}{\lambda_i+d-i+1},
```

with top eigenspace $`S_\lambda(\ker H)`$.

## 2. Highest-weight occupation identity

Fix a supported partition. Set $`a_k=\lambda_k-\lambda_{k+1}`$ for $`1\le k\le d-1`$. Realize $`V_\lambda`$ as the highest-weight component inside

```math
\mathcal W_\lambda
=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{d-1}\bigl(\Lambda^k\mathbb C^d\bigr)^{\otimes a_k},
\qquad
\Omega=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{d-1}(e_1\wedge\cdots\wedge e_k)^{\otimes a_k}.
```

This component is generated by the unit highest-weight vector $`\Omega`$. For $`a_k>0`$, define $`O_k(H)`$ as the compression to $`V_\lambda`$ of the sum of one-body copies of $`H`$ over the $`a_k`$ wedge factors of degree $`k`$. On a single wedge the one-body sum is the restriction of $`\sum_{j=1}^{k}H^{(j)}`$ to antisymmetric tensors. Thus $`O_k(H)\succeq0`$ when $`H\succeq0`$, and

```math
\langle\Omega,O_k(P_{e_i})\Omega\rangle=a_k\,\mathbf 1_{\{i\le k\}}.
```

The equivariant complex-linear maps from $`\operatorname{End}(\mathbb C^d)`$ into $`\operatorname{End}(V_\lambda)`$ are spanned by $`H\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(H)I`$ and the maps $`O_k`$ with $`a_k>0`$. To see the dimension, move tensor factors in the space of intertwiners to identify it with $`\operatorname{End}_{U(d)}(V_\lambda\otimes\mathbb C^d)`$. The Pieri rule gives one inequivalent summand for adding a box in the first row and one for adding it in row $`k+1`$ for each $`a_k>0`$. All summands occur once. The displayed maps are independent because their matrix elements on $`\Omega`$ at the coordinate projectors give a constant vector and the independent step vectors with jumps at the active $`k`$. Taking matrix elements on these $`d`$ projectors therefore determines $`T_H^\lambda`$.

Let $`s_i=\langle\Omega,T_{P_{e_i}}^\lambda\Omega\rangle`$. The measure with density $`(D_\lambda/r_\lambda)\langle\Omega,\Pi_\lambda(f)\Omega\rangle`$ has mass one, and $`s_i`$ is its mean of $`|f_i|^2`$. The rank-one determinant formula on each wedge factor gives

```math
\langle\Omega,\Pi_\lambda(f)\Omega\rangle
=\prod_{k=1}^{d-1}
\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^k |f_i|^2\right)^{a_k}.
```

Indeed, the action on the wedge of $`e_1,\ldots,e_k`$ has diagonal matrix element equal to the determinant of the restriction of $`I-P_f`$ to their coordinate span. For a Haar ray the squared amplitudes are uniform on the simplex. The fractions $`Y_i=|f_i|^2/(1-\sum_{j<i}|f_j|^2)`$, $`i<d`$, are independent with laws $`\operatorname{Beta}(1,d-i)`$. The likelihood multiplies their densities by $`\prod_{i<d}(1-Y_i)^{\lambda_i}`$. The resulting means of $`|f_i|^2=Y_i\prod_{k<i}(1-Y_k)`$, with $`Y_d=1`$, are

```math
s_i=\frac{1}{\lambda_i+d-i+1}
\prod_{k<i}\frac{\lambda_k+d-k}{\lambda_k+d-k+1},
\qquad
\frac{s_{i+1}}{s_i}
=\frac{\lambda_i+d-i}{\lambda_{i+1}+d-i}
\quad (1\le i<d).
```

The ratios are at least one and exceed one exactly when $`a_i>0`$. Thus the step coefficients give the operator identity

```math
T_H^\lambda
=s_d\operatorname{tr}(H) I_{V_\lambda}
-\sum_{k:a_k>0}\frac{s_{k+1}-s_k}{a_k}\,O_k(H).
```

It implies $`T_H^\lambda\preceq s_d I`$ for a guessed ray. The sum of the $`O_k(H)`$ is the compression of the total occupation of that ray. Since it is the infinitesimal action of $`e^{itH}`$, it preserves $`V_\lambda`$. For rank-one $`H`$ its spectrum is nonnegative and integral, and its kernel is $`S_\lambda(\ker H)`$. All active coefficients in the identity are positive, which identifies that kernel as the top eigenspace of $`T_H^\lambda`$. If $`N=0`$, the one-dimensional space is already the kernel of the zero occupation operator.

## 3. Optimal POVM and an exact finite implementation

On a supported block, $`B_H`$ equals $`r_\lambda T_H^\lambda/[D_\lambda(d-1)^N]`$ tensored with the multiplicity identity. Let $`Z`$ be scalar on each block, given by replacing $`T_H^\lambda`$ by $`s_d I`$ there, and zero on unsupported blocks. The inequality $`B_H\preceq Z`$ holds for every reported ray. POVM completeness bounds mean fidelity by $`\operatorname{tr}Z`$, which is the sum in the risk formula.

The covariant density at ray $`g`$ is

```math
E_g
=P_{\mathrm{null}}
+\bigoplus_{\lambda:\lambda_d=0}
\frac{D_\lambda}{r_\lambda}\Pi_\lambda(g)\otimes I_{M_\lambda},
```

where $`P_{\mathrm{null}}`$ projects onto the unsupported sectors. Each outcome effect is supported within the zero-deficit space of $`Z-B_{P_g}`$. Schur's lemma gives $`\int E_g\,dg=I`$. The density attains the Haar upper bound, and covariance gives the same fidelity for every $`f`$. It therefore attains the minimax value. On each supported sector, the construction is the marginal of [Keyl's highest-weight measurement](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0412053) obtained by reporting only the last ray of the basis. Averaging the highest-weight projector over the basis choices at fixed last ray gives $`\Pi_\lambda(g)/r_\lambda`$ by irreducibility for $`U(d-1)`$.

An exact finite POVM uses a positive quadrature for ray polynomials of degree $`t=N+1`$ in $`P_g`$. With quadrature nodes $`g_b`$ and weights $`w_b`$, take effects $`w_b E_{g_b}`$ and report $`g_b`$. The entries of $`E_g`$ are polynomials of degree at most $`N`$ in $`P_g`$, since $`\Pi_\lambda(g)`$ is a block of $`(I-P_g)^{\otimes N}`$. For each true $`f`$, the fidelity integrand multiplies these entries by one additional degree through $`\operatorname{tr}(P_g P_f)`$. The quadrature therefore preserves both completeness and pointwise fidelity.

One explicit rule uses the $`d-1`$ independent fractions $`Y_i\sim\operatorname{Beta}(1,d-i)`$ and $`d-1`$ relative phases. On each phase take the uniform grid of $`t+1`$ roots of unity. Haar and grid averaging both remove all unbalanced monomials of degree at most $`t`$. Balanced monomials are polynomials of degree at most $`t`$ in each $`Y_i`$, so choose $`\lceil(t+1)/2\rceil`$ point Gauss–Jacobi quadrature for each corresponding beta law. The product formula has positive weights and at most $`[(t+1)\lceil(t+1)/2\rceil]^{d-1}`$ outcomes. This is an ideal finite POVM specification. Implementation uses the ability to retain and measure all $`N`$ specimens together.

## 4. When a fully joint measurement first helps

**Up to $`d-1`$ specimens.** Measure each specimen in an orthonormal laboratory basis. Report any ray $`g`$ orthogonal to all resulting basis vectors. Such a ray exists for $`N<d`$. Each product effect lies in $`(g^\perp)^{\otimes N}`$, inside the top score eigenspaces after Schur decomposition. The procedure therefore reaches the bound on Haar mean fidelity. A common Haar laboratory rotation, applied to the bases and the reporting rule and retained as a classical setting, gives the same fidelity for every true ray. This establishes equality of the one-copy and joint minimax risks.

**At $`d\ge3`$ specimens.** Here the only unsupported sector is the rank-one fully antisymmetric projector $`Q`$ onto $`\Lambda^d\mathbb C^d`$. For $`H=P_g`$, the top subspaces of all supported sectors form $`(g^\perp)^{\otimes d}`$. By the occupation identity and covariance, there exists a common strictly positive spectral gap $`\Delta_d`$ such that

```math
Z-B_{P_g}\succeq
\Delta_d\bigl(I-(I-P_g)^{\otimes d}-Q\bigr)
```

for every guess $`g`$. For an explicit choice, minimize $`r_\lambda(s_{k+1}-s_k)/[D_\lambda(d-1)^d a_k]`$ over supported partitions and active $`k`$. The total occupation is at least one off its kernel, so this choice follows from the occupation identity. The two subspaces being removed are orthogonal. Although the source never populates $`Q`$, every complete POVM must account for its full trace.

A biseparable effect is a positive sum or limit of operators whose pure product terms factor across a nonempty cut. Different terms can use different cuts. Take a unit product vector across a $`k\mid d-k`$ cut and let $`x,y`$ be the probabilities of no occurrence of $`g`$ in the two factors. Its weight in $`(g^\perp)^{\otimes d}`$ is $`xy`$. The alternating unit vector has uniform Schmidt coefficients $`1/\sqrt{\binom dk}`$ across the cut, indexed by complementary wedges of a basis containing $`g`$. Split the Schmidt terms according to the side containing $`g`$. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the product vector's weight $`b`$ in $`Q`$ by

```math
b\le
\frac{\bigl(\sqrt{(1-x)y}+\sqrt{x(1-y)}\bigr)^2}{\binom dk}
\le\frac2d(1-xy).
```

It follows that its deficit expectation is at least $`\Delta_d(d/2-1)b`$. The same trace inequality holds for every biseparable effect by linearity. Summing the effects gives $`\int\operatorname{tr}[E(dg)Q]=\operatorname{tr}Q=1`$, so the best Haar fidelity for such a POVM lies at least $`\Delta_d(d/2-1)`$ below the collective optimum. The same strict gap applies to its worst-case fidelity.

When the receiver keeps only a classical transcript between measured groups, the effect associated with each complete history is a tensor product across the groups in that history. If every group uses fewer than $`d`$ specimens, each history is biseparable, including adaptive choices of the groups. Thus, at the first budget where collective readout can help, a receiver with exclusively such groups falls short of the fully joint optimum. The [three-qutrit hierarchy](measurement-hierarchy.md) resolves the finer comparison among the measurement classes in dimension three.

## 5. Small budgets and fixed-dimension limit

For qutrits one can write $`\lambda=(N-j,j,0)`$ with $`0\le j\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor`$. Its probability and its conditional fidelity are

```math
p_j=\frac{(N-2j+1)}{2^N}
\left[\binom Nj-\binom N{j-1}\right],
\qquad
s_3=\frac{(j+1)(N-j+2)}{(j+2)(N-j+3)},
```

with $`\binom N{-1}=0`$. This gives collective risks $`5/8,\ 23/40,\ 21/40,\ 67/140,\ 783/1792`$ for budgets one through five. For $`d=2`$ there is just the single-row partition and the formula recovers pure-qubit risk $`1/(N+2)`$.

At fixed dimension, Weyl's dimension formula bounds $`r_\lambda`$ by a polynomial in $`N`$. The tableau count satisfies $`m_\lambda\le N!/\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\lambda_i!`$, since an assignment of entries to rows specifies at most one standard tableau. It follows that the sector weights are dominated by a polynomial times the uniform multinomial probability with $`d-1`$ categories. For each fixed $`\varepsilon>0`$, the mass with any $`|\lambda_i/N-1/(d-1)|>\varepsilon`$ decays exponentially. On a fixed neighborhood of the balanced vector with all coordinates bounded away from zero,

```math
N(1-s_d)=\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\frac{N}{\lambda_i+d-i+1}+O_d(N^{-1}).
```

Concentration makes the expectation converge to $`(d-1)^2`$. The complement makes a vanishing contribution because $`0\le N(1-s_d)\le N`$. This limit holds at fixed $`d`$ for the ideal all-specimen receiver and the stated uniformly occupied source.
